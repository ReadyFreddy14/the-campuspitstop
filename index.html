THE CAMPUSPITSTOP · UNT DENTON PILOT

Business plan answers

A working proposal for a fuel and essentials stop serving UNT commuters. Verified facts are linked. The site, prices, sales, and costs below are assumptions until tested with students, suppliers, and a property owner.

01 · EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

One useful stop on a busy route

TheCampusPitStop would combine regular fuel, drinks, snacks, quick meals, and everyday school supplies near UNT’s Denton campus. Student drivers are the first customer group; nearby residents and employees are secondary. The proposed advantage is completing a fuel-and-essentials trip close to class. Sales would come from fuel and in-store purchases. A campus-card option would require university and payment-provider approval; no UNT partnership is in place.

02 · COMPANY & PURPOSE

Why, how, what

Mission: help busy students handle essential stops with less travel and time pressure. Vision: a convenient, financially sustainable stop near a campus that students choose for everyday needs. Why: commuting and tight schedules can make basic errands costly in time and money. How: test a site on a student driving route, stock frequent needs, and compare total trip value with existing options. What: fuel and convenience retail. The proposed service area is the W Eagle Drive corridor near UNT; an actual parcel, owner, permits, and campus approval have not been secured.

03 · PRODUCTS & SERVICES

Fuel plus daily essentials

Drivers would buy regular gasoline at the pump and shop a compact selection of drinks, grab-and-go food, snacks, and supplies in store. The example model tests $3.59 per gallon and a $9 average in-store basket; individual product prices need supplier costs and shelf-price checks. Debit/credit and cash are proposed; campus-card acceptance is contingent on approval. A quick combined trip supports the mission, while fuel-only students can use the pump without buying anything else.

04 · INDUSTRY & MARKET

A real commuter segment, demand still to test

UNT reported 42,016 students in fall 2026 across its enrollment, including online and Frisco students. UNT’s Commuter Care Scholarship identifies fuel and transportation costs as student challenges. This supports investigating Denton commuters, but not a 42,016-person customer pool. A traffic count, student survey, and route analysis are needed before committing to the assumed 396,000 annual gallons.

05 · TARGET CUSTOMERS

Start with student drivers

Primary: students who drive to UNT Denton and need fuel between class, work, and home. The student uses and decides on the purchase; the student or a family supporter may pay. Secondary: nearby residents, employees, and visitors who pass the site. They decide and pay for their own purchases. Students who do not drive may buy snacks but have strong existing campus dining options. The forecast assumes about 1,085 gallons and 63 in-store transactions per day on average; these are testable volume assumptions, not observed UNT buying habits.

06 · COMPETITIVE ADVANTAGE

Compete on the whole trip

QuikTrip on W Eagle Drive and Sam’s Club on W University Drive are direct fuel alternatives. UNT’s Market by Clark Bakery is an indirect alternative for snacks and ready-to-eat food. Sam’s listed $3.669 regular on Sept. 28, with membership required; current pump prices at the other fuel station were not verified. The proposed differentiator must be a conveniently placed combined stop, tested against actual detour time and final price, not an unsupported cheapest-price claim.

07 · BUSINESS MODEL

Two revenue streams, thin fuel spread

Fuel is the larger sales stream; in-store transactions provide a second source of gross profit. The hypothetical year has 396,000 gallons at $3.59 and 23,100 store transactions at $9. That produces $1,421,640 fuel sales and $207,900 store sales. Direct costs are assumed at $3.23 per gallon and $5.40 per store transaction. Monthly expenses include $16,000 payroll, $5,000 rent, $800 marketing, and $2,200 other. Pump count, operating hours, summer and winter breaks, and staffing may constrain these volumes. The example reaches a $480 operating profit in October (month 10), but still loses $62,280 over Year 1; see the monthly forecast.

Porter’s Five Forces: effect and response

Rivalry · High

QuikTrip and other stations already combine fuel and food. Compare route time, total receipt, and product availability weekly; adjust offerings instead of claiming an unverified price lead.

New entrants · Moderate

Fuel sites require capital, approvals, and suitable land, but another convenience store could enter. Secure a feasible site and build repeat visits before expanding.

Substitutes · High

Students can fuel elsewhere and buy snacks through UNT Dining. Test whether one combined stop saves enough time to change habits.

Buyer power · High

Drivers can compare pump signs and change stations easily. Show the final price clearly and gather feedback on the convenience benefit.

Supplier power · High

Fuel supply, land, insurance, and equipment are material costs. Seek competing quotes and avoid promising a discount before the spread is viable.

Year 1 answer

Illustrative sales: $1,629,540. Direct costs: $1,403,820. Gross profit: $225,720. Ongoing operating expenses: $288,000. Projected operating result: $62,280 loss. The 12-month table shows seasonal volumes and an annual total. This is before one-time startup costs, borrowing costs, and taxes.

What would validate the plan?

Count traffic at a viable parcel; survey UNT student drivers about route, frequency, and basket; obtain fuel and product supplier quotes; confirm land use and permits; price insurance, equipment, and a lease. GOOD QUESTIONS

Before you hit
the road.

Can I use scholarship money for fuel?

This is a proposed feature, not an available payment method. Eligibility depends on your specific award and school’s rules. We would need approval from participating schools and scholarship providers before enabling it. Ask your financial aid office about your award.

Is there already a station on my campus?

CampusPitstop is a concept preparing for a pilot. No campus locations, opening dates, or institutional partnerships have been confirmed.

Can alumni get a discount too?

Yes, alumni membership is part of the proposed model. Verification, discounts, and final membership terms will be confirmed with launch partners.

How could my campus participate?

Our proposed starting point is a small pilot with a campus and an experienced fuel operator. Campus interest would help shape the location, student demand, and service model before a full station launch.

UNT PILOT · RESEARCH AND WORKING ASSUMPTIONS

What we know, and what still needs proof

UNT-area demand

UNT reported 42,016 students in fall 2026 across its enrollment, including students at Frisco and online. That is a broad university audience, not the number of likely customers at a Denton fuel site. UNT News, Sept. 11, 2026

UNT recognizes transportation costs for commuting students through its Commuter Care Scholarship and describes commuter parking options. These establish a real commuter segment, but they do not prove that students would use this store or buy the forecast volume.

Research still needed: site traffic counts, student surveys, commute and shopping patterns, fuel supplier quotes, and permission for a proposed site.

Customer segments to test

  • Primary: UNT Denton student drivers. They use and usually pay for fuel; a parent may fund some purchases. Convenience before or after classes is the proposed benefit.
  • Secondary: nearby residents, employees, and visitors. They could buy fuel or essentials if the site is on their route.
  • Food substitute: students without cars can already buy snacks and meals at UNT retail locations, so the store must offer a reason to choose it.

Segment size, visit frequency, and willingness to pay remain survey questions.

Demand math and break-even test

The annual 396,000 gallons equal about 33,000 per month. At an assumed 12 gallons per fill-up, that is about 90 fills per day over 365 days. The 23,100 store transactions equal about 63 per day. These are planning volumes, not survey findings. Summer months are lower in the monthly table; the model assumes the station can serve more than 120 fills per day in peak months.

At the assumed 36¢ fuel contribution per gallon and $3.60 store contribution per transaction, the average month generates $18,810 gross profit against $24,000 operating expenses. October assumes 43,000 gallons and 2,500 store purchases: $15,480 + $9,000 − $24,000 = $480 operating profit. Repeating October every month would be needed to sustain break-even; one positive month does not repay the Year 1 loss or startup investment.

What the research can and cannot establish

The linked UNT sources establish enrollment and the existence of commuter transportation needs. The named competitors establish real alternatives, and the dated Sam’s Club listing is one comparison price. None proves the proposed location can sell 396,000 gallons or 23,100 store baskets in Year 1. No founder-run student survey, traffic count, lease quote, fuel supply quote, or contractor bid is available here. The presentation should describe those as next validation steps, never as completed research.
